The real problem

The pressure doesn’t stay at work.

If the way you respond stays the same, so does the conversation you keep avoiding.

You put off the hard talk.

You know what needs saying. Another week goes by, and your team is left guessing.

You go quiet when it gets tense.

You have something useful to say, but by the time you find the words, the meeting is over.

You bring it home.

The conversation ended hours ago. You’re still thinking about what you could have said.

The method

Three days built on one distinction.

The gym and the lodge work together. A guided physical challenge gives you something real to notice. In the lodge, you talk through where the same response shows up at work or at home.

A wall

External, static, defensive. You build it to keep people out and it ends up keeping you in. Under pressure you cover up and retreat. The room gets smaller.

A boundary that travels

Not something you build. It is what you can handle, so it goes where you go. Under pressure you hold your ground and move. The room stays yours.

Leading people means being firm and warm at the same time, without giving up either one. That is the hard part. Three days of practice is how it starts to stick.

No boxing experience is required. You choose your intensity. The aim is to notice what you do when you feel uncomfortable, then choose one way to respond differently next time.

Questions
